# Seiko Presage Collection — Comprehensive Technical Knowledge Base

## Product Identity

### Collection Name
**Seiko Presage** — A collection that combines Japanese aesthetic sense with traditional craftsmanship and Seiko's mechanical watchmaking skills, delivering quality and performance in dress/lifestyle automatic watches.

### Brand Heritage
- **Founded:** Seiko Corporation, Japan (est. 1881; 145th anniversary in 2026)
- **Presage Line Launched:** 2016 (internationally), inspired by the 1913 Seiko Laurel — Japan's first wristwatch
- **Positioning:** Mid-range mechanical dress watch collection; bridge between entry-level Seiko 5 and premium Grand Seiko
- **Target Market:** Watch enthusiasts, professionals, and collectors seeking Japanese mechanical craftsmanship at accessible price points

### Price Range
- **Entry Level (Cocktail Time / Style60s):** $420–$670 USD
- **Mid Range (Sharp Edged / Japanese Garden):** $814–$1,100 USD
- **Premium (Classic Series):** $950–$1,400 USD
- **Craftsmanship Series (Enamel, Porcelain, Urushi):** $794–$2,134 USD
- **Limited Editions / Anniversary:** Up to ~$5,000 USD
- **Average:** ~$500–$1,500 USD for most models

## Movement Technologies (TRIMATIC)

Seiko's proprietary TRIMATIC system used across Presage movements comprises three technologies:

### 1. Diashock
Shock-resistance structure protecting balance wheel pivots and bearings from impact damage.

### 2. Magic Lever
Patented efficient automatic winding mechanism (invented 1959). Uses two spring pullets of different shapes in constant contact with the transmission wheel:
- **Downstroke:** Pull pullet rotates transmission wheel; push pullet slides
- **Upstroke:** Push pullet rotates transmission wheel; pull pullet slides
- **Result:** Bidirectional rotor motion converted to unidirectional mainspring winding
- **Advantage:** Fewer parts than Swiss-style winding systems; more efficient energy capture from wrist movement

### 3. SPRON
Proprietary alloy used for mainspring (Spron 510) and hairspring:
- Resistant to tearing, corrosion, and wear
- Ensures long-term accuracy and reliability
- Mainspring variant: Spron 510

## Case & Materials

### Case Materials
- **Primary:** Stainless steel (316L, ~150–200 HV)
- **Coatings:** DiaShield super-hard coating (~500 HV on Vickers scale; ~1.5x harder than conventional coatings)
- **Plating Options:** Yellow gold, rose gold, dual-tone
- **Titanium:** Available in select models (e.g., SARX055 "Baby GS Snowflake")

### Case Finishing
- **Zaratsu Polishing:** Distortion-free mirror finish; surfaces polished to be completely flat without optical distortion (hallmark technique shared with Grand Seiko)
- **Hairline Finish:** Fine linear brushing on flat surfaces
- **Combination:** Polished sides contrasting with brushed flat surfaces for multi-dimensional effect

### DiaShield Super-Hard Coating
- **Process:** Highly ionized metals combined with nitrogen, carbon, and oxygen ions in vacuum to create harder alloy coating
- **Hardness:** ~500 HV (Vickers); compared to 150–200 HV for standard 316L stainless steel
- **Purpose:** Scratch resistance preservation of case beauty
- **Limitation:** Surface coating only; once scratched through, cannot be polished back
- **Super Black Variant:** ~1.5x hardness of conventional hard coatings

## Dial Materials & Craftsmanship

### Standard Dial Techniques
- **Sunray finish:** Radiating lines creating color variation as light moves
- **Embossed/pressed patterns:** Textured surfaces (Asanoha hemp leaf, Karesansui garden raking)
- **Iridescent finish:** Color-shifting effect depending on viewing angle
- **Guilloche-like patterns:** Machine-pressed decorative textures

### Artisan Dial Techniques (Craftsmanship Series)

#### Urushi Lacquer
- **Technique:** Hand-applied natural lacquer, repeated painting and polishing
- **Heritage:** Edo period (1603–1868)
- **Master Craftsman:** Isshu Tamura
- **Characteristics:** Deep, rich, luminous surface; each dial unique

#### Arita Porcelain
- **Technique:** Fired porcelain dial, both glazed and unglazed variants
- **Heritage:** Arita porcelain manufacturing since 1830
- **Master Craftsman:** Hiroyuki Hashiguchi
- **Characteristics:** Pure white (glazed) or warm matte texture (unglazed); extremely thin and delicate

#### Enamel
- **Technique:** Vitreous enamel coating fired onto metal dial
- **Master Craftsman:** Mitsuru Yokosawa
- **Thickness:** 0.1mm (flawless application)
- **Colors:** White, green, blue
- **Characteristics:** Depth, purity, and brilliance of color impossible with paint

#### Shippo Enamel
- **Technique:** Multi-step process of glazing, firing, and polishing (unique wave pattern)
- **Master Craftsman:** Wataru Totani
- **Characteristics:** Distinctive wave pattern visible after polishing

### Dial Colors (by Sub-Collection)
- **Sharp Edged:** Aitetsu (indigo iron blue), Tokiwa (evergreen green), Susutake (brown), Shironeri (unbleached white), Sumiiro (black ink), Crimson Red
- **Classic Series:** Shiroiro (white), Sensaicha (tea), Araigaki (persimmon), Sumiiro (black), Aijiro (indigo), Fushiiro (wisteria), Aiiro (indigo blue)

## Maintenance & Care

### Service Interval
- **Recommended:** Every 3–5 years for full overhaul (cleaning, lubrication, adjustment)
- **6R35 Caliber Specific:** Every 2–3 years
- **Baseline Overhaul Cost:** ~$260 USD (through authorized Seiko service)
- **Includes:** Movement cleaning, lubrication, adjustment, gasket replacement, water resistance testing

### Water Resistance
- Have water resistance professionally tested annually if frequently exposed to water
- Do not operate crown or pushers when watch is wet or submerged
- Rinse with fresh water after saltwater exposure

### Magnetism Avoidance
- Keep away from strong magnetic fields: speakers, refrigerators, mobile phones, laptop speakers, magnetic clasps
- Standard magnetic resistance: 4,800 A/m (60 gauss) for 6R-series movements
- If magnetized: Have demagnetized by authorized service center

### Manual Winding Procedure
1. Remove watch from wrist
2. With crown in normal (pushed-in) position (Position 0)
3. Rotate crown clockwise approximately 20–30 turns
4. For 6R35: 55+ turns for full mainspring wind
5. This provides sufficient power to start and sustain the movement

### Crown Positions (6R35 Example)
- **Position 0 (Normal):** Manual winding (clockwise rotation)
- **Position 1 (First click out):** Date adjustment (counterclockwise rotation)
- **Position 2 (Second click out):** Time setting (both directions)

### General Care
- Wipe regularly with soft, dry cloth to prevent oxidation from dust, moisture, or perspiration
- Avoid extreme temperatures (operating range: 5–35 C)
- Store in watch box or on watch winder when not worn for extended periods
- Avoid strong shocks (drops, impacts)

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|:---|:---|
| Asanoha | Hemp leaf geometric pattern used on Sharp Edged dials; dates to Heian period |
| Karesansui | Japanese dry landscape (Zen garden) with raked gravel patterns |
| Urushi | Traditional Japanese natural lacquer, hand-applied in multiple layers |
| Arita Porcelain | Fine Japanese porcelain from Arita town, Saga Prefecture; tradition since 1616 |
| Shippo | Japanese cloisonne enamel technique with distinctive wave patterns |
| Zaratsu | Mirror polishing technique producing distortion-free flat surfaces |
| DiaShield | Seiko's proprietary hard coating for scratch resistance (~500 HV) |
| TRIMATIC | Seiko's three core movement technologies: Diashock + Magic Lever + Spron |
| Magic Lever | Seiko's efficient bi-directional automatic winding mechanism (patented 1959) |
| Diashock | Shock-absorbing structure protecting balance wheel pivots |
| Spron | Proprietary alloy for mainspring and hairspring |
| LumiBrite | Seiko's non-radioactive luminescent paint |
| Hardlex | Seiko's proprietary mineral crystal glass |
| Hacking | Feature where seconds hand stops when crown is pulled for precise time setting |
