# 1. Product Overview

## 1.1 Description

The Seiko Presage is a collection of automatic mechanical watches that embodies the intersection of Japanese aesthetic sensitivity, centuries-old artisan craftsmanship, and Seiko's proprietary movement technology. Launched internationally in 2016, the Presage line draws inspiration from the 1913 Seiko Laurel — Japan's first wristwatch — and positions itself as the bridge between the entry-level Seiko 5 and the premium Grand Seiko.

Every Presage watch is powered by a self-winding mechanical movement featuring Seiko's TRIMATIC technology: Diashock impact protection, the Magic Lever efficient winding system, and SPRON proprietary alloys for mainsprings and hairsprings. These movements convert the natural motion of the wearer's wrist into stored mechanical energy, requiring no battery or external power source.

The collection is distinguished by its dial artistry, ranging from machine-pressed textures inspired by Japanese patterns (Asanoha hemp leaf, Karesansui garden raking) to handcrafted dials using centuries-old techniques — Urushi lacquer, Arita porcelain, and vitreous enamel applied by named master craftsmen. Case finishing includes Zaratsu polishing — a distortion-free mirror technique shared with Grand Seiko — and DiaShield super-hard coating for scratch resistance.

## 1.2 Key Features

- **Automatic Mechanical Movement** — Self-winding via Seiko's Magic Lever; no battery required; 41–72 hour power reserve depending on caliber
- **Japanese Artisan Dials** — Machine-pressed textures and handcrafted Urushi lacquer, Arita porcelain, and enamel dials by master craftsmen
- **Zaratsu Polishing** — Distortion-free mirror finish on case surfaces, a hallmark technique shared with Grand Seiko
- **DiaShield Coating** — Super-hard surface treatment (~500 HV) for scratch resistance on Sharp Edged and select models
- **Sapphire Crystal** — Scratch-resistant sapphire with inner anti-reflective coating on premium sub-collections
- **Exhibition Caseback** — See-through caseback revealing the decorated automatic movement
- **Hacking Seconds** — Seconds hand stops when crown is pulled for precise time setting
- **GMT / Dual Time** — Available across multiple sub-collections for tracking two time zones
- **LumiBrite** — Seiko's proprietary non-radioactive luminous paint on select models

# 2. System Architecture

## 2.1 Mechanical Movement Overview

[IMAGE: Exploded view technical illustration of a Seiko Presage automatic movement (Caliber 6R35) showing all major components separated vertically — from top to bottom: crystal, dial with hands, movement plate with train wheels visible, automatic winding module with rotor (Magic Lever mechanism highlighted), mainspring barrel, balance wheel with hairspring, and caseback. Each component labeled with leader lines. Clean white background, technical illustration style with subtle shadows showing depth. Cross-section detail inset showing the Magic Lever mechanism with pull and push pullets]

The Seiko Presage automatic movement converts wrist motion into stored mechanical energy through a self-winding mechanism:

1. **Energy Capture** — The oscillating rotor (visible through the caseback) swings with wrist movement
2. **Magic Lever Winding** — Seiko's patented Magic Lever converts bidirectional rotor motion into unidirectional mainspring winding using two spring pullets
3. **Energy Storage** — The mainspring (SPRON 510 alloy) stores energy in the barrel, providing 41–72 hours of power reserve
4. **Regulation** — The balance wheel oscillates at 21,600 or 28,800 beats per hour, regulated by the SPRON hairspring
5. **Timekeeping** — The gear train transmits regulated energy to the hour, minute, and second hands
6. **Protection** — Diashock system absorbs impacts to protect the delicate balance wheel pivots

## 2.2 TRIMATIC Technology

Seiko's proprietary TRIMATIC system comprises three core technologies used across all Presage movements:

| Technology | Function | Benefit |
|:-----------|:---------|:--------|
| **Diashock** | Shock-resistance structure protecting balance wheel pivots | Impact protection from drops and knocks |
| **Magic Lever** | Patented bidirectional automatic winding mechanism (1959) | Efficient energy capture from natural wrist movement; fewer parts than Swiss equivalents |
| **SPRON** | Proprietary alloy for mainspring (Spron 510) and hairspring | Resistance to tearing, corrosion, and wear; long-term accuracy |

## 2.3 Crown Positions

| Position | Action | Operation |
|:---------|:-------|:----------|
| **Position 0** (pushed in) | Manual winding | Rotate crown clockwise |
| **Position 1** (first click out) | Date adjustment | Rotate counterclockwise |
| **Position 2** (second click out) | Time setting | Rotate in either direction; seconds hand stops (hacking) |

::: {.notice}
Crown positions may vary slightly between caliber families. Refer to the caliber-specific instruction sheet included with each watch.
:::

# 5. Operation

## 5.1 Starting the Watch

If the watch has stopped (power reserve depleted):

1. **Remove** the watch from storage.
2. **Hold** the watch face-up in one hand.
3. **Rotate** the crown clockwise (Position 0 — pushed in) approximately 20–30 turns.
   - For 6R35 and 6R5x calibers: 55+ turns for a full mainspring wind.
4. **Place** the watch on your wrist. The rotor will maintain the charge through your natural wrist movement.

::: {.tip}
If you do not wear the watch daily, consider storing it on a watch winder to maintain the power reserve and keep the date current. Alternatively, manually wind the crown 20–30 turns before wearing.
:::

## 5.2 Setting the Time

1. **Pull** the crown to Position 2 (second click out). The seconds hand stops (hacking function).
2. **Wait** for a time reference (phone, radio, TV) to reach the desired second.
3. **Rotate** the crown in either direction to set the correct time.
4. **Push** the crown back to Position 0 when the reference reaches the target second.

## 5.3 Setting the Date

::: {.caution}
**DATE MECHANISM RISK** — Do not adjust the date between 9:00 PM and 2:00 AM. During this period, the date-change mechanism is engaged and forcing the crown can damage the movement gears.
:::

1. **Pull** the crown to Position 1 (first click out).
2. **Rotate** the crown counterclockwise to advance the date.
3. **Push** the crown back to Position 0 when the correct date is displayed.

## 5.4 GMT Function (GMT Models Only)

GMT-equipped models (Caliber 4R34, 6R54, 6R64) feature an independent 24-hour hand for tracking a second time zone:

1. **Pull** the crown to the GMT setting position (varies by caliber — consult specific instruction sheet).
2. **Rotate** the crown to move the 24-hour GMT hand independently.
3. **Set** the GMT hand to the desired second time zone using the 24-hour scale on the dial or bezel.
4. **Push** the crown back to Position 0.

::: {.notice}
The GMT function is a "caller/office" type — the 24-hour hand is independently adjustable while the local time hands continue running. This allows you to track a home time zone while travelling.
:::

## 5.5 Power Reserve

| Caliber Family | Power Reserve | Wearing Recommendation |
|:---------------|:-------------|:----------------------|
| 4R series | ~41 hours | Wear daily or wind every 2 days |
| 6R35 | ~70 hours | Wear daily or wind every 3 days |
| 6R51/6R55/6R5J | ~72 hours | Wear daily or wind every 3 days |
| 6R27/6R64 | ~45 hours | Wear daily or wind every 2 days |

::: {.tip}
Models with the 6R-series 70–72 hour power reserve can maintain timekeeping through a full weekend off the wrist without manual winding.
:::

# 6. Maintenance

## 6.1 Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency | Procedure |
|:-----|:----------|:----------|
| Wipe case and bracelet | Weekly | Clean with a soft, dry cloth to remove moisture and perspiration |
| Rinse after saltwater | After each exposure | Rinse thoroughly with fresh water; dry completely |
| Check bracelet clasp | Monthly | Verify push-button release functions correctly |
| Water resistance test | Annually | Professional test at authorized Seiko service center |
| Full movement overhaul | Every 3–5 years | Complete disassembly, cleaning, lubrication, adjustment, gasket replacement |

## 6.2 Calibration (Accuracy)

### 6.2.1 Expected Accuracy

| Caliber Family | Daily Accuracy | Notes |
|:---------------|:--------------|:------|
| 4R series | -35/+45 seconds per day | Entry-level; measured at 5–35 °C |
| 6R series | -15/+25 seconds per day | Premium; measured at 5–35 °C |

::: {.notice}
Real-world accuracy depends on wearing habits, wrist position during sleep, ambient temperature, and magnetic field exposure. If accuracy consistently exceeds the rated range, have the watch inspected at an authorized Seiko service center.
:::

### 6.2.2 Factors Affecting Accuracy

| Factor | Effect | Mitigation |
|:-------|:-------|:-----------|
| Temperature extremes | Rate changes outside 5–35 °C range | Avoid prolonged exposure to extreme heat or cold |
| Magnetism | Hairspring magnetization causes rate gain | Keep away from speakers, phones, laptop speakers, magnetic clasps (see *Section 6.3*) |
| Shock / impact | Balance wheel pivots can shift | Avoid drops and impacts; Diashock provides basic protection |
| Position | Rate varies between dial-up, crown-up, etc. | Normal; average across wearing positions |
| Low power reserve | Rate decreases as mainspring unwinds | Wear daily or wind regularly |

## 6.3 Magnetism Avoidance

::: {.caution}
**MAGNETISM RISK** — Strong magnetic fields can magnetize the hairspring, causing the watch to gain several minutes per day. The 6R-series movements have magnetic resistance of 4,800 A/m (60 gauss), but stronger fields can still affect accuracy.
:::

**Keep the watch at least 5 cm away from:**

- Mobile phone speakers
- Laptop speakers and keyboards (magnets in lid closure)
- Tablet covers with magnetic clasps
- Refrigerator magnets
- Audio speakers
- Magnetic jewelry clasps
- Induction cooktops

**If magnetized:** Have the watch demagnetized at an authorized Seiko service center. This is a quick, non-invasive procedure.

## 6.4 Overhaul Service

A full movement overhaul should be performed every 3–5 years (every 2–3 years for 6R35 caliber):

**What the overhaul includes:**

- Complete movement disassembly
- Ultrasonic cleaning of all components
- Replacement of worn parts
- Re-lubrication with fresh oils and greases
- Timing adjustment and regulation
- Gasket replacement (crown, caseback, crystal)
- Water resistance testing
- Final accuracy testing across multiple positions

**Estimated cost:** ~$260 USD through authorized Seiko service.

# 7. Troubleshooting

## 7.1 Common Issues

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Quick Checks | Fix | Escalate When |
|:--------|:-------------|:-------------|:----|:--------------|
| Watch has stopped | Power reserve depleted | Check if crown is in Position 0 | Wind crown clockwise 20–30 turns; wear on wrist | Watch stops again within 24 hours of full wind |
| Gaining several minutes/day | Magnetized hairspring | Place near compass — if needle deflects, watch is magnetized | Have demagnetized at service center | Gaining persists after demagnetization |
| Losing more than rated accuracy | Low power reserve or worn movement | Wind fully; wear for 24 hours; measure deviation | Wind daily; if still inaccurate, service needed | Deviation exceeds rated range consistently |
| Date not changing | Crown in wrong position; or adjusting during 9PM–2AM | Verify crown is in Position 1 for date | Advance time past midnight to trigger date change | Date mechanism jams — requires service |
| Rotor not spinning | Winding mechanism issue | Hold watch and tilt — rotor should swing freely | Visible through caseback | Rotor stuck or not spinning freely |
| Condensation under crystal | Gasket failure; water ingress | Check crown is pushed in fully | Bring to service center immediately — moisture damages movement | Any visible condensation |
| Crown difficult to pull out | Normal resistance / debris | Ensure screw-down crown is fully unscrewed first (if applicable) | Gently pull with dry fingers | Crown does not pull out at all |
| Bracelet clasp not closing | Clasp mechanism worn | Inspect clasp for visible damage or debris | Clean clasp area; apply gentle pressure | Clasp does not lock securely |

## 7.2 When to Contact Service

Contact an authorized Seiko service center when:

- Watch consistently loses or gains time beyond the rated accuracy range
- Date mechanism does not advance at midnight
- Crown feels loose, grinds, or does not return to Position 0
- Water or condensation is visible under the crystal
- Crystal is cracked or chipped
- Rotor sound changes (grinding, scraping instead of smooth whirring)
- DiaShield coating shows deep scratches to base metal
- Bracelet links or clasp mechanism fails

# 8. Safety Information

## 8.1 General Precautions

::: {.caution}
**SKIN IRRITATION** — Prolonged contact with moisture, perspiration, or dirt trapped between the watch and skin can cause irritation. Clean the caseback and bracelet regularly. Remove the watch if irritation occurs and consult a physician if symptoms persist.
:::

- Do not expose the watch to temperatures outside 5–35 °C for extended periods
- Avoid strong shocks and impacts (drops from height, striking hard surfaces)
- Remove the watch before vigorous sports activities that may cause impacts
- Do not place the watch near strong magnets or electronic devices with magnets

## 8.2 Water Resistance Precautions

::: {.caution}
**WATER DAMAGE** — Water resistance is not permanent. Gaskets deteriorate over time with exposure to perspiration, cosmetics, and temperature changes. Have water resistance tested annually at an authorized service center.
:::

- **Never** operate the crown when the watch is wet or submerged
- **Never** press pushers underwater (if equipped)
- Rinse with fresh water after saltwater or chlorinated water exposure
- Avoid hot water (showers, hot tubs, saunas) — thermal expansion can compromise seals
- 50 m (5 bar) rating: Splash-resistant only — not suitable for swimming
- 100 m (10 bar) rating: Suitable for swimming and water sports — not for scuba diving

## 8.3 Chemical Exposure

- Avoid contact with solvents, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes — these can damage gaskets, coatings, and strap materials
- Remove the watch before applying hand sanitizer, sunscreen, or insect repellent
- Leather straps are particularly vulnerable to moisture and chemicals — keep dry
